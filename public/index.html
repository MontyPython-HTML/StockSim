Your very own LLM-Based Stock Simulation

vision

Anyone should be able to learn how markets behave without paying the market for the lesson. The prices, the signals and the bills are real; the money is simulated, and so is the future that begins where the history ends.

The mission

Build the teacher, not just the terminal. Every stock you hold is watched for the patterns traders actually read — golden crosses, RSI extremes, volume spikes — and explained on your own chart, with that bar's numbers, the moment it appears.

Architecture

Under the Hood

Five moving parts, front to back: one tick loop, two possible sources for a price, an agent layer, one store, and a screen that reads them all through a single crosshair.

The Session Engine

One tick is one trading day. Bills settle, dividends pay out, the AI queue drains and a new bar reaches the page — and the routes stay thin, because every rule lives in one place.

Real or Generated Prices

A session replays stored history until it runs out, then rolls onto its own seeded future. Both sit behind one interface, so nothing downstream knows which one it is reading.

Agent Layer

Gemini answers through an MCP server — market events, pattern lessons, a coach's read — so the model is a tool the engine calls rather than a service it waits on.

TigerData Store

Prices, sessions, trades, dividends and events live in TimescaleDB, loaded by CLI ingestion and written in batches so a tick costs a handful of round trips.

One Crosshair

Price, RSI and MACD share one crosshair, with the basket rebased to 100 and your equity curve beside them. All five panels run off a single play, pause and speed clock.

Investment
Analysis

Nothing on the game screen is decorative. Every tick recomputes the indicators traders actually read, prices the news against the names it genuinely touches, and keeps what your picks earned separate from what the bank paid you.

1. Price Action and Signals

Every bar carries SMA-20/50, RSI-14, MACD and 20-day average volume, computed on the same pass as the close — so the crossover on your chart is the number the coach quotes back at you.

2. Scoped News Events

A company headline reprices one symbol, a sector headline every name you hold in that sector, and a macro headline the whole basket — because five semiconductor names are not five bets.

3. Return Attribution

Trading return and total return are reported side by side, so dividends, salary and bills never blur into how well your picks actually did.

we hit real problems building this, and designed around them

Model
Reliability

Challenge: Headlines and lessons come from a language model that can take seconds to answer, rate-limit under load, or not be configured at all — and the simulation clock cannot afford to stop and wait on it.

Mitigation

Async with local fallback

Remote
Database

Challenge: Prices, sessions and trades live in a TimescaleDB instance across the network, so a tick costs round trips rather than CPU — a naive rebuild spent 24 to 29 queries on every tick.

Mitigation

Cached, batched writes

Rewriting
History

Challenge: A generated future is seeded, so it can be re-rolled on demand — but the player has already traded against those candles, and regenerating them would silently rewrite decisions already made.

Mitigation

Seed locked at the fork
